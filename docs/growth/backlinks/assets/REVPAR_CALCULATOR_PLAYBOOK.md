# RevPAR Calculator Playbook

Status: Draft  
Owner: Norixo  
Program: Growth

## Purpose

The RevPAR Calculator is a strongly linkable Norixo asset because RevPAR is repeatedly used as the practical metric that connects rate and occupancy. The completed discovery waves show that publishers frequently explain its formula, compare it with ADR and occupancy, provide an example, or discuss the limits of using a single performance metric.

The calculator is most appropriate when it helps a reader apply an existing explanation. It is not a substitute for a publisher’s own definition, methodology, benchmark or product documentation.

## Best Opportunity Types

| Opportunity type | Fit | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Calculator Reference | Primary | This is the most direct fit when an existing page explains the RevPAR formula and readers benefit from applying it. It was one of the two most frequent opportunity types in the first two waves. |
| Guide Citation | Primary | Suitable for evergreen revenue-management, pricing and KPI guides that explain the relationship between RevPAR, ADR and occupancy. It was equally frequent in the observed sample. |
| Revenue Management Glossary | Strong | A maintained glossary can direct readers from a concise definition to a practical calculation without duplicating the glossary entry. The pilot produced one qualified example of this pattern. |
| KPI Library or Educational Resource | Strong | Appropriate where a structured educational section covers core performance metrics together. The calculator should be presented as a learning continuation, not as a product claim. |
| Resource Page | Selective | Appropriate only when the page is curated, maintained and already serves a defined educational need. The opportunity mapping classifies it as a medium-difficulty, medium-scalability format. |
| Methodology Citation | Selective | Use only when the page’s subject is metric interpretation, data provenance or conventions. In most such contexts, Research Methodology is the primary asset; the calculator is complementary rather than the default. |
| Documentation Reference | Conditional | A fit where product documentation teaches users how to interpret RevPAR, provided the external resource can add neutral clarification and the publisher’s proprietary convention is not conflated with Norixo’s. |

## Recommended Editorial Angle

Use factual, reader-centred angles that correspond to a page’s existing content:

- **Definition:** clarify that RevPAR relates accommodation revenue to available inventory over the relevant period.
- **Formula:** support a reader who wants to calculate RevPAR from accommodation revenue and available nights, or interpret the ADR × occupancy relationship when conventions match.
- **Interpretation:** explain why RevPAR should be read with ADR and occupancy rather than in isolation.
- **Available-inventory treatment:** make the page’s handling of blocked, owner or unavailable nights explicit; do not assume terminology is equivalent across publishers.
- **Limitations:** preserve the distinction between revenue efficiency and profitability; RevPAR does not by itself represent operating margin or all revenue streams.
- **Benchmarking discipline:** frame comparisons around like-for-like periods, property types and conventions, not a universal “good” RevPAR.

Do not use a marketing claim, a generic tools-list insertion, a promise of better results, or an unsupported universal benchmark.

## Evidence From Pilot and Sprint 1

The existing discovery records identify four opportunities recommending the RevPAR Calculator:

| Evidence | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|
| A maintained vacation-rental encyclopedia entry | Readers of a RevPAR definition may benefit from a practical calculation reference, especially where the entry distinguishes RevPAR from ADR and occupancy. |
| A detailed RevPAR explainer with calculation, example and FAQ structure | Evergreen explanatory content is a strong fit for a calculator reference or guide citation. |
| A vacation-rental revenue-management glossary | A concise glossary can be a suitable bridge to a calculator when it already explains the relationship between rate, occupancy and available inventory. |
| A dedicated calculation and comparison guide | Formula-led pages with worked figures, availability treatment and discussion of limitations are the clearest observed Tier A environment for the asset. |

Additional cross-wave evidence supports the asset indirectly: the two most frequent opportunity types were Calculator Reference and Guide Citation, and the portfolio analysis found that the KPI cluster, RevPAR Calculator and Research Methodology each appeared four times. The evidence base is directional: it reflects 30 reviewed domains and must not be treated as a link-acquisition forecast.

## Qualification Priorities

### Tier A

Prioritise maintained, evergreen pages that:

- teach the RevPAR formula or a worked calculation;
- explain RevPAR alongside ADR and occupancy;
- distinguish revenue from profitability or describe metric limitations;
- state their own convention for revenue and available inventory; and
- provide a clear reader need for applying the metric.

The preferred formats are Calculator Reference and Guide Citation. A glossary or knowledge-base entry can qualify where it is maintained and educational.

### Tier B

Consider pages that are materially relevant but require a later check, including:

- broader revenue-management guides where RevPAR is one metric among several;
- vendor documentation with product-specific definitions such as adjusted occupancy or RevPAN;
- association education sections without an observed external-resource pattern; and
- specialist-media explainers where current editorial accessibility is uncertain.

These opportunities require an explicit convention comparison and a page-maintenance review before they progress.

### Tier C

Keep for selective review only:

- time-bound news articles reporting a RevPAR movement;
- unmaintained roundups, generic best-tools lists or pages without a calculation need;
- purely commercial landing pages; and
- pages where an external reference would duplicate a complete existing explanation without adding reader value.

## Risks

- **Content duplication:** a calculator reference is not justified if it repeats an adequate formula, example and interpretation without adding an application need.
- **Weak incremental value:** a general tool mention does not meet the editorial-fit standard.
- **Obsolescence:** news, quarterly reports and older content may be unsuitable even when they mention RevPAR.
- **Convention mismatch:** RevPAR, RevPAN, adjusted occupancy, room revenue and available-night definitions can differ. Never imply equivalence without confirming the page’s definitions.
- **Revenue-versus-profit confusion:** avoid positioning RevPAR as a direct measure of profitability.
- **Commercial context:** vendor documentation and blogs may be relevant but require a neutral, non-reciprocal rationale and a publisher-policy review.
- **Over-concentration:** the first evidence base is KPI-focused. Do not infer that every revenue-management page merits the same asset.

## Future Outreach Preparation

Before any future contact, collect and verify only the information necessary to qualify the specific page:

1. Confirm the page remains public, current and editorially maintained.
2. Record the exact RevPAR definition, formula and included/excluded inputs used by the publisher.
3. Confirm the page’s audience, format and observable reason why a calculator would improve reader understanding.
4. Determine whether the page contains external references or an observable editorial update mechanism.
5. Establish whether the most appropriate asset is the RevPAR Calculator, the broader KPI Calculators, or Research Methodology.
6. Document evidence, qualification status, priority and any convention mismatch in the opportunity inventory before beginning contact discovery.
7. Apply the existing stop rules, publisher guidelines and commercial-neutrality standards before any outreach.

This playbook does not create a campaign, identify a prospect, define a message, or authorise outreach.
