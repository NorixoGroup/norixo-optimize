# Editorial Opportunity Qualification Framework

Status: Draft  
Owner: Norixo  
Program: Growth

## Purpose

This framework defines the mandatory qualitative review of one editorial opportunity before any outreach action. Its purpose is to protect editorial credibility, avoid irrelevant requests, preserve publisher trust, and ensure that Norixo assets are proposed only where they create independent value for the audience.

This document is a method, not a portfolio, contact list, campaign plan, scoring model, or outreach template.

## Qualification Dimensions

### Editorial Relevance

Definition: the degree to which the opportunity naturally belongs in the publisher’s editorial scope.  
Why it matters: relevance is the minimum condition for a durable editorial reference.  
Qualitative evaluation: verify that the opportunity topic is routinely and credibly covered.

### Audience Fit

Definition: the match between the publisher’s audience needs and the educational value of the proposed asset.  
Why it matters: a link must improve the reader experience, not merely serve Norixo.  
Qualitative evaluation: identify a concrete reader question the asset helps answer.

### Asset Fit

Definition: the suitability of one Norixo asset for the exact opportunity type and context.  
Why it matters: an otherwise strong publisher is unsuitable if the proposed asset is not the best resource.  
Qualitative evaluation: confirm that the asset provides a direct, non-duplicative next step.

### Authority Potential

Definition: the expected credibility and editorial significance of a reference from the opportunity.  
Why it matters: Norixo prioritises trusted, relevant citations over volume.  
Qualitative evaluation: assess the publisher’s reputation, editorial standards and sector role without relying on a single metric.

### International Reach

Definition: the relevance of the opportunity beyond one narrowly local audience or market.  
Why it matters: international reach can strengthen the durability and multilingual value of an asset.  
Qualitative evaluation: determine whether the content is locally specific, regionally useful or broadly reusable.

### Content Freshness

Definition: whether the relevant editorial area is current, maintained or reasonably likely to be updated.  
Why it matters: stale content reduces the value and likelihood of a meaningful editorial improvement.  
Qualitative evaluation: review publication cadence, update signals and topic recency.

### Link Sustainability

Definition: the likelihood that a legitimate editorial reference would remain available and contextually relevant.  
Why it matters: durable references are more valuable than temporary placements.  
Qualitative evaluation: prefer maintained guides, documentation, research libraries and established editorial formats.

### Editorial Accessibility

Definition: the observable ability of a publisher to update content, accept contributions or cite external sources.  
Why it matters: a highly relevant opportunity may be impractical if no editorial route exists.  
Qualitative evaluation: inspect visible publication patterns and contributor or citation practices; do not infer internal policy.

### Relationship Potential

Definition: the potential for a respectful, long-term professional relationship based on mutual editorial value.  
Why it matters: the program seeks trusted relationships rather than one-off link transactions.  
Qualitative evaluation: assess whether the organisation’s public activity, formats and values suggest a credible long-term fit.

### Commercial Neutrality

Definition: the ability to position the proposed asset as an educational reference rather than a commercial placement.  
Why it matters: neutral framing protects publisher trust and reduces manipulation risk.  
Qualitative evaluation: confirm that the proposed contribution is useful even if it generates no commercial outcome.

### Research Value

Definition: the contribution of methodology, data literacy, definitions or evidence to the opportunity.  
Why it matters: research value is especially important for public, academic and analytical contexts.  
Qualitative evaluation: identify whether a transparent methodological explanation materially improves the context.

### Topical Alignment

Definition: the consistency between the opportunity’s topic and Norixo’s documented authority areas.  
Why it matters: alignment prevents drift into unrelated, low-quality placements.  
Qualitative evaluation: verify a direct relationship to accommodation performance, revenue management, market intelligence, listing optimisation or closely related hospitality knowledge.

## Qualification Outcome

| Status | Meaning | Permitted next step |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified | Evidence supports a relevant, neutral and sustainable editorial opportunity. | Prepare a page-level opportunity review; no contact is implied. |
| Needs Review | The opportunity appears plausible but material evidence is incomplete or ambiguous. | Obtain the missing evidence before any action. |
| Not Suitable | The opportunity lacks editorial, audience, asset or topical fit. | Do not pursue. Preserve the decision rationale. |
| Blocked | A policy, ethical, legal, safety or conflict concern prevents progress. | Do not pursue unless the blocking condition is formally resolved. |

## Evidence Required

Before an opportunity can be Qualified, retain qualitative evidence that demonstrates:

- an observable editorial format compatible with the opportunity type;
- a credible thematic connection to the proposed subject;
- a defined audience need;
- a clear reason why one Norixo asset improves the audience experience;
- visible signs of content maintenance or a durable publication format;
- absence of an apparent link-selling, manipulation or low-quality-content pattern;
- an opportunity framing that remains useful without a commercial exchange;
- no known conflict with publisher guidelines, public policy or Norixo principles.

## Qualification Checklist

Use this checklist for each opportunity before assigning an outcome.

- [ ] The opportunity type is defined using `EDITORIAL_OPPORTUNITY_MAPPING.md`.
- [ ] The editorial topic is directly relevant to the publisher’s observable scope.
- [ ] The audience need is specific and documented qualitatively.
- [ ] One Norixo asset is selected because it is genuinely useful, not merely available.
- [ ] The proposed asset is current, public and appropriate for the context.
- [ ] The opportunity is framed as an editorial improvement rather than a link request.
- [ ] The source shows no apparent link farm, PBN, automated-content or undisclosed paid-placement pattern.
- [ ] The intended reference could remain useful if the commercial relationship never develops.
- [ ] The opportunity does not conflict with policy, legal, privacy or reputation constraints.
- [ ] The evidence is sufficient to explain the decision to an independent reviewer.

## Decision Rules

1. Mark an opportunity **Qualified** only when editorial relevance, audience fit, asset fit, topical alignment and commercial neutrality are all clearly supported by evidence.
2. Mark an opportunity **Needs Review** when any required evidence is missing, ambiguous or contradictory.
3. Mark an opportunity **Not Suitable** when the proposed asset does not materially improve the editorial context, even if the publisher appears authoritative.
4. Mark an opportunity **Not Suitable** when relevance depends on an artificial insertion, generic resource page or reciprocal-link logic.
5. Mark an opportunity **Blocked** when link-selling, manipulation, privacy, legal, safety or reputational concerns are identified.
6. Do not override a Blocked status through authority, traffic, geographic reach or commercial appeal.
7. Record the evidence and rationale for every outcome so that the decision is reviewable and reproducible.

## Escalation Rules

Manual review is required when:

- the opportunity involves a public institution, academic organisation or regulated subject;
- the evidence indicates a potential commercial, sponsorship or disclosure issue;
- the opportunity concerns statistics, research, methodology or claims requiring source validation;
- the target context is multilingual and meaning could change through translation;
- the publisher’s editorial policy is unclear or conflicts with the apparent opportunity;
- a potential reputational, legal, privacy or safety concern exists;
- there is disagreement over the appropriate asset, opportunity type or qualification outcome.

## Governance

- This framework is the single qualification standard for future editorial opportunities.
- Changes require documentation of the rationale and compatibility with previous decisions.
- Qualification outcomes must be evidence-based, reviewable and retained with their decision rationale.
- The framework must remain independent of outreach volume, commercial pressure and unverified authority metrics.
- Periodic review should focus on consistency of decisions, emerging manipulation risks and changes in publisher practices.
